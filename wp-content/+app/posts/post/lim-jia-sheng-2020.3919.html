This was my 2nd hosted website ever, fresh off my discovery of Lottie as a technology. Imagination was running rampant in the air, & I don't own a gasmask. At this point of my life, I'd been an amatuer motion designer for 6+ years, & amassed enough hours in Adobe After Effects to have trauma & sentiment attached to it. Hence, the moment I found out what Lottie could do, I imagined abusing it to create something far greater than what my experience on the web had let me accomplish up till that point.

"2nd website? What about the 1st?"
Well unfortunately that's lost to time, from disk failure to unrenewed servers (est. 2016). Though rest assured you're not missing much.

Though behind the scenes, prior to the inception of this site, I'd been ambiently sharpening my trade with code through various small snippets of code. From ExtendScript (analogous to JavaScript ES3, Flash developers iykyk) in After Effects expressions, to little, obnoxious Windows Batch (cmd) scripts. However, never had I thrown myself into a project that required actual coding grunt, & this site posed to be the first boss fight of my web development journey.

All that talk about development, but as primarily a designer by trade, that was what I started this project out aiming to smash out the park. The first sections I worked on were the main "who am_i?" & "what_now?", which anchored the styling going forward. Whilst there, I knew that I wanted a few quirky bits, like the accenting eye-candy we're all used to in traditional mograph.

Of course, a site needs to have content, right? I had heard about how after college, we'd need to have an "e-portfolio" site that required us to blog about our work. The looming threat was to use the, at that time already considered "legacy", platform Blogger. It was that, or other HTML-based rich-text web builders online.

Adobe Muse--a discontinued visual web builder that was to replace Dreamweaver--was my main experience building for the web at that point, & I knew the pain of fanangling HTML without actually being able to see the HTML. Hence, markdown was the only option when formulating a blog.

On the technical side, the site was built with a custom animation engine! This really let me get into the weeds of API design early, & forced me to adopt TypeScript somewhere in the middle of the project. TypeScript came at a time where I faced a lot of issues tracking what functions needed as arguments, & the propaganda really sold it for me. Once in a blue moon, maybe society is right about an agenda, & I still stand by it now. TypeScript's magic made the difference in helping me complete the project, which kickstarted the whole development journey & brought this article to your eyeballs. Unfortunately this also thoroughly began my OOP era, but we won't talk about that.

Reflection

At the end of the day, I'm still really proud of this project. It wasn't easy, but it was really fun. I'm really sentimental about it because it brings back a simpler time, where Rollup & Vanilla were still completely viable stacks. To add salt to the wound of modern web tech, this might literally be the site with the best Lighthouse scores in my portfolio. May all new developers stumble upon a project like this one that pushes them to fall into the rabbit hole, paragliding their way into great problem solutions & playful stressors.
