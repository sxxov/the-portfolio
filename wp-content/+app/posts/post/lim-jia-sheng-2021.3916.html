So, here came an opportunity to one-up myself, as midway through college, I was given a task to create a new site. This foretold the prophecy of online blogging & grading for my higher education experience in lockdown, as not only was this site graded, it would host the writeups for future tasks too.

Around this time, I'd decided that I wanted to really get into web development & do it properly. Like clockwork, as a junior developer, it came time to choose a framework & be obnoxious about it. After doing some research, it was obvious that React was the dominant choice, even at the time. Though, people were well-aware of React's downsides, & they were aggressively pinning it against the up & coming tech--Vue & Svelte. I picked Svelte, but not because I really understood what I was getting into, but because it was supposedly fast (it is), & its syntax was the cleanest (pre-v5 redesign).

Hence, to try out my shiny new toy, I wanted to really push the technical side of the site, this time around.

Terminal

As a teen who spent way too much time playing around with the Windows Command Prompt (& subsequently Batch script), I wanted to carry over the magic I felt typing in commands to my own visual environment.

Collage

To show off my existing work, I had the idea of a collage. However, I didn't want to create one myself. What if I could procedurally generate one from a set of images, & what if it would be different for every user? On the technical side, this would start a small excursion into creatively using bitmaps, CSS grid, & clips, to achieve a tetris-esque section of my work. A concave-hull algorithm was used to calculate the polygon, which would occasionally produce weirdly incorrect results (it's a feature now). Emojis were also added, because of course they were cool then.

Call-to-action

Again with the procedural theme, this time, picking copy from a bag of text.

Contact

This little game I thought was the greatest idea ever, especially the UX part. Since you had to spam click the "enemy" to get to the prompt, it also meant you were likely to click through into the email link by accident, opening your email client immediately.

Portfolio

Finally, for the actual reason this site exists, the host for work. To make it a bit more fun, & awfully unscalable, the portfolio was done like a point & click adventure game. Though truth be told, it was a little bit because I didn't have much time to create the rest of this site--assignments man.

"What's with the massive header?"
This was when Samsung's One UI design was still cutting edge, & the large headers there stood as inspiration for this. Though, less for usability & more for the fact that it's cool, especially when it would collapse.

Reflection

This was the first "website" project I did, & if you couldn't tell, I had a great time. Svelte was great, & it opened the doors to these newly interactive parts of the site I could have never made without it--at least cleanly. The only thing I would change if I could go back & restart, is just to reduce the cringeness of the copy in the About page (do not read). Other than that, it's a good stepping stone for my next sites.
